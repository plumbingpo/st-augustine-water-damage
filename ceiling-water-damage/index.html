Ceiling Water Damage Repair in St. Augustine

A brown ring on the ceiling is the last thing to show up, not the first. Before any mark shows, water has traveled down a joist, saturated the insulation and collected on top of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. If the cavity above is still wet, the mark comes back and mold can start on the paper backing within 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Signs to Look For

  • Ceiling texture crumbling off or changing color in one section
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • A ceiling panel that droops, bulges or gives under light pressure
  • A smoke detector, register or fixture with water running from it
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

Hours 24 to 48

Stains and Bubbles

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. Mold is established in the cavity and the repair now covers a much larger area.

Our Process

How We Handle Ceiling Water Damage Repair

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. Until the water stops coming, fixing the ceiling would not last.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Open and Dry

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Repair and Finish

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Sudden Collapse

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

Wet Lights and Wiring

The damp area usually includes fan mounts, recessed fixtures and wiring boxes. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

Growth on the Hidden Side

Mold usually grows out of sight on the upper face of the panel and in the insulation above, both of which hold moisture well after the painted side dries.

Older Ceiling Materials

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. If your home has one, it should be tested before anyone scrapes, cuts or tears out a damaged area.
