Burst Pipe Emergency Guide in St. Augustine, FL

Home / Blog / Steps to Take When a Pipe Bursts in St. Augustine, FL

Few plumbing problems rattle a homeowner like a pipe that suddenly bursts. A single burst pipe can release 4 to 8 gallons of water per minute, which can flood rooms, ruin belongings, and weaken structural materials within hours. Act correctly in those opening minutes and you may spare yourself thousands of dollars in losses, turning a potential major restoration job into a repair you can manage. Below, St. Augustine Restoration lays out a step by step plan that homeowners in St. Augustine, FL can follow when it happens.

Step 1: Shut Off the Water Supply Immediately

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Start by finding the main shutoff valve for the house. In most St. Augustine homes, it sits near the water meter, typically in the garage, basement, utility room, or along an exterior wall near the street. To stop water throughout the house, twist the handle clockwise (righty-tighty) until it stops. If you can identify the specific pipe that burst and it has its own shutoff valve nearby, close that valve as well.

Your water company can cut the supply at the meter, so call them at once if the valve is missing or will not budge. This is one of the most important reasons to know your shutoff valve location BEFORE an emergency. Go look for it today, then check that the handle turns without a fight. If it's stuck, have a plumber service it before you need it in an emergency.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

Before the crew arrives, you can cut your losses, starting with getting documents, electronics, and valuables out of the wet zone. Furniture resting on damp carpet should be raised, with foil or plastic blocks set under each leg. Remove area rugs from wet floors. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Air needs to circulate, so leave cabinets and closets open. If you can reach the breaker panel safely and stay out of the water, switch off power to the affected rooms.

Do not step into pooled water anywhere close to outlets, electrical panels, or appliances. If the burst pipe has caused significant flooding and you cannot safely access your electrical panel, leave the electricity on and wait for professionals to assess the situation. Safety always comes first.

Step 4: Record the Damage for Your Claim

Before you begin cleaning up, document the damage thoroughly for your insurance claim. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. Together with the detailed reports from St. Augustine Restoration, your records back up the claim and help you pursue a fair settlement.

Lowering Your Burst Pipe Risk in St. Augustine

While not all pipe bursts can be prevented, St. Augustine homeowners can significantly reduce their risk by insulating exposed pipes in unheated areas like garages, attics, and crawl spaces. During cold weather, keep faucets dripping slightly to maintain water movement. For sinks on outside walls, leave the cabinet doors beneath them open so warm air gets to the plumbing. Pressure over 80 PSI calls for a pressure reducing valve, so check your reading from time to time. Replace aging supply lines, especially rubber washing machine hoses, with braided stainless steel. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
