Water Heater Leak Cleanup in St. Augustine: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

Our St. Augustine team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWater from the tank can scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
How We Fix It

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Check Around the Heater

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Drying and Moisture Readings

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. We record moisture readings and tell you when the space is ready for the new heater.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • The drain valve at the bottom of the tank cracking, loosening or failing to shut fully.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.

Garage Wall Base

Water crossing a garage slab soaks the bottom plate and drywall of the wall shared with the house, then moves into the flooring on the living side.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Flooring type in the neighboring rooms, where the water reached them.
  • The amount of wall drywall, ceiling drywall or insulation that has to be removed.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • The volume of wet stored items that must be dried, moved or disposed of.
