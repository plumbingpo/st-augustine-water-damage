Crawl Space Water Removal: What St. Augustine Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. You might notice it as a door that suddenly rubs or a floor that is no longer level.

Weakened Floor Framing

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Water That Is Not Clean

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

The pump out itself is normally done in a few hours. Expect 3 to 5 days after that for the lumber, with dehumidification and air movement going the whole time. It can run longer when the beams are large, the ground is uncovered, or the flooding went unnoticed for weeks. We go by moisture readings, not by the calendar.
Mold can begin on damp wood and paper faced insulation within 24 to 48 hours. Because the area is unlit and has almost no airflow, it seldom dries without help. The sooner the water is out and the air is dry, the less mold has to work with. Where growth has already started, we clean the wood and treat it with an antimicrobial.
It is best to stay out as long as there is standing water. Electrical cables and boxes are common down there, and wet ones can energize the water. Add the chance of contamination from drains or soil, limited room to move and bad air quality. Shine a light in from outside the opening if you like, and leave the rest to a trained crew.
Some wet soil following a big storm happens often, yet water that pools is a problem and needs attention. Mold and wood rot both follow when as little as one or two inches sit there raising the humidity. If water is still there a day after the rain stops, or shows up in dry weather, have it checked.
For a little clean water, a shop vac is fine. Joists and subfloor stay wet, though, since a box fan only pushes moist air in circles. Crawl spaces are also tight, dark and may have live wiring or sewage in the water. Professional pumping and drying is the safer choice once the water is more than a thin puddle.
