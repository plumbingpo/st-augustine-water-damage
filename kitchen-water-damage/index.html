Kitchen Water Damage: What St. Augustine Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. Our St. Augustine crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Stale, damp air that hits you when the doors under the sink are opened
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

The Ice Maker Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
