How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water from a floor above is streaming down interior walls.
  • A water heater tank has failed and is emptying across the floor.
  • Standing water is spreading across floors toward other rooms or down the stairs.
  • You have no way to locate or get to the shutoff while water continues to pour.
  • Trapped water overhead has made part of the ceiling bulge or sag.
  • Several hours or more have passed with hardwood, drywall or carpet sitting wet.
  • An outlet, a light fixture or the ceiling above has water dripping out of it.

Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Run a check on the sump pump ahead of wet weather, and think about adding battery backup.
  • Know where your electrical panel is so power to wet rooms can be cut safely.
  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces so they do not freeze and split in cold weather.
  • Before anything goes wrong, store the phone number for an emergency restoration crew.
  • Store valuables and important papers off the floor, especially in basements and lower levels.
  • Going away longer than a few days? Close the main water valve first.

Why Emergency Water Damage Restoration Is Not a DIY Job

Mopping the puddle in front of you is rarely the real challenge during an emergency. What takes skill is figuring out where everything else has soaked in. Water runs under baseboards, into wall cavities and beneath flooring within minutes. Meters and thermal imaging show us the wet spots hidden from view, which means they get handled that night rather than surfacing weeks on as a stain or a smell.

Our night crew arrives with gear homes simply do not have, including commercial air movers, properly sized dehumidifiers and truck-mounted extractors.
