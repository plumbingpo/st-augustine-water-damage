Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in St. Augustine

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. From there it tops the edge of the basin and runs out over the slab. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerIf the breaker panel can be reached while you stand somewhere dry, switch off the circuits for the basement. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceSee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • An intake screen packed with mud and stones so the pump cannot draw water.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Stored Belongings

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Porous items left sitting in the water resist disinfecting and grow mold in a matter of days.

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Silty water also gets caught below vinyl plank and laminate, which normally means lifting them.

Drywall and Wall Insulation

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Our Sump Pump Failure Cleanup Process

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Wearing protective gear, we take out the carpet, pad, wet drywall and insulation that the groundwater touched, bag it and haul it away so clean framing and concrete are exposed.

3

Clean and Sanitize

The slab, wall framing and remaining contents are washed down and treated with an antimicrobial product. Drying comes afterward, because fans on a dirty basement would blow contaminants everywhere.

4

Dry and Confirm

We run drying equipment until our meters show normal moisture in the slab and the wood, which typically takes 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • How contaminated the water is, because the category drives disposal and disinfection.
  • If there are finished walls and floors, the tear-out of drywall, trim and carpet.
  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.
  • How many stored belongings have to be moved, cleaned or thrown away.
